Current campaign

South Portland Tank Farms

There are 120 large petroleum tanks in South Portland, run by six major oil companies. Residents have raised concerns for decades that the tanks compromise air quality for the people who live, work and play very close to them. New state data measuring air quality along the fencelines of these tank farms has confirmed what we’ve felt, and smelled, for years: They are bad neighbors, profiting off harm to our community.

Concerning readings were collected near all the tanks, but two tank farms stand out. Measured against Maine’s ambient air guidelines, the benchmark the state uses for lifetime exposure, benzene came in above the guideline at ten of the state’s eleven petroleum terminals. The worst readings in Maine are here: South Portland Terminal on Lincoln Street, bordered by businesses, homes and Forest City Cemetery, where the long-term average ran as high as four and a half times the guideline, and Citgo on Mechanic Street in Pleasantdale, where the monitors sit directly against homes. Now is the time to act:

  • The council is weighing what to require at the tank farms. Staff and special counsel are returning to the December workshop with options to choose from:
    • stricter emission controls, through the state and DEP
    • a local ordinance or licensing approach
    • an interim 500-foot building moratorium near Citgo and South Portland Terminal
    • additional monitoring closer to those facilities, and in the Shipyard area
    • a fresh review of Potential to Emit, the permitted ceiling rather than the actual total
  • The Council plans its next workshop for Tuesday, Dec. 1 at 6:30 p.m. in City Hall Council Chambers, 25 Cottage Road. Arrive by 6:15 to fill the room.
  • Email the City Council and ask it to require better technology at the tank farms, and to make public what special counsel advises about the city’s authority. City Council email addresses are found here: southportland.gov/411/City-Council.
  • Email protectsouthportland@gmail.com to get plugged into local tank farm efforts.
